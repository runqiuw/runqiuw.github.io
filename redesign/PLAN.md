## Decisions

| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| Hosting | Astro, GitHub Actions → GitHub Pages, same repo and URL |
| Page ↔ room | **The page says what it is; the room shows the thing itself.** The page is complete on its own (also the whole mobile site); the room never repeats it and shows what the page can't for the item in view. No captions or hints in the room panel |
| Clarity rule | Everything a professor needs (name, position, research, papers, CV, email) is readable without touching the room |
| Home layout | Desktop: room fixed on the left (~40–45%), page scrolls on the right. Mobile: list page plus an "Explore my room in 3D" card |
| Room behavior | Scroll: the section in view picks the object and camera shot, the item in the middle of the viewport picks what it shows. Hover/focus shows that item, never moves the camera. Click or "Enter my room" opens the full-screen room |
| Views (2026-10-22) | Three levels: page (`#`), full-screen room (`#room`), close-up (`#room/<id>`). Forward goes down one level: "Enter my room" or a click on the panel opens the room; in the room, a click on an object or its label opens its close-up, or goes to its page section if it has none yet. Back always goes up one level, and Esc, the Back buttons and the browser's back all do the same thing. A close-up with a page section has "<Section> on the page". Close-ups are config (`CLOSEUPS` in `room.config.ts`): `desk` (desktop details; a click on a monitor leaves the room for the paper it shows, or for the terminal line clicked), `board` (drag pinboard cards; a click on a card leaves the room for that card on the page), and `hoops` (below). A close-up without a page section has no "on the page" button; its object is still clickable and labeled by the close-up id. `RoomPanel.astro` owns the levels and the history; `room.ts` only flies the camera (`focus`/`unfocus`/`visit`) and runs each close-up's interaction |
| Visual language | "Toy Room": clay shapes, Fredoka + Nunito, electric palette in light mode, "lights off" in dark mode (same switch as the room lights), spring motion, full-color photos |
| CV | PDF only, linked in the intro and nav; `/cv/` redirects to it |
| Games | Their own corner in the room; cards say what each game is |
| Name audio | Labeled as an AI voice |
| Hoops (2026-10-24) | Bin at plan (3, 0.15) m, 1.25 cm from the wall; the backboard target’s lower edge meets the rim. Compact 1.40 × 1.68 m court rug/collider, 1.32 m three-point arc; cart restored beside the bedside backpack, clear of the court. Paper → bottle → bouncy ball rotates automatically. Shuffled deck of 24 real throwing spots, ~0.59–3.55 m: close shots, both baselines, six bed positions, four extra deep threes and five explicit low/near-level cameras. Chair-side release moved clear of the chair. Static colliders include the floor, ceiling, walls, bed, rug, chair and major furniture; an outlined held model must not intersect furniture. The actual release remains at canvas (50%, 62%); shorten the camera dolly along its ray near walls, adapt shoulder offset/height, keep the rim inside portrait frames, and clear furniture with the eye. Held objects have one thin mint silhouette (~1.8 CSS px), expanded in clip space from welded/smoothed selection geometry. A stencil mask excludes the full interior, including transparent glass and cap/label seams; no separately enlarged parts, dark keyline, idle spin or post-processing. Release removes the highlight; resize updates its pixel-width viewport. Backboard tape uses opaque alpha cutout with alpha-to-coverage, and the rim writes depth for correct occlusion. Pull down for power (22% of canvas short side), sideways for direction; 52° elevation normally, 40–42° for the deepest shots to clear the ceiling. Up to 16 fading guide dots use matching cannon-es prediction, show the first 82% before contact, omit impact/bounce solutions. Keep the view during flight. A made basket or any non-backboard contact ends the attempt; floor/furniture/wall hits close scoring, while a rim rattle may still drop in during the result hold. Backboard hits must be within its real bounds, otherwise they are terminal wall hits. After the result, hold 1.4 s (full callout animation), then move for 650 ms and ready the next item. An 8 s flight watchdog handles invalid/stalled states, not normal round timing; old throws cannot score after a new round. Rim entries checked at every 1/120 s substep count once. +2 mint single ring/small burst; +3 gold/pink double ring/larger burst. Four random callout pools (2/3 × direct/bank), no immediate phrase repeat. Last 10 throws stay and sleep, with a 10 s post-launch fidget limit. Respect reduced motion. Lazy `room-hoops.ts` shares cannon-es with bed toys; Blender rug generator `bin/room_court_asset.py` stays in the normal export pipeline |

Not wanted: chat-style home page, room-only navigation, heavy real-time scenes.

Room links: recognize `#room` and configured `#room/<id>` on first load, reload and same-page hash navigation, including a cold mobile load. ### Performance budget
- Performance baseline tools (2026-10-28): `redesign/PERFORMANCE-TOOLS.md` documents profiling, WebGL allocation estimates and deterministic same-device image comparisons. The local ignored baseline covers 102 room images, including real cached map assets, detail crops and 64 motion frames; two serial captures matched across all 120 image/crop comparisons. A separate instrumented 1× M5 memory run estimated a 372.9 MB room peak with 4× canvas MSAA and no unknown formats; this is not measured VRAM or directly comparable with the earlier 354 MB estimate. Controlled camera frames do not replace real drag/physics checks.
- Text first: static HTML, LCP never waits for 3D. three.js loads on idle on desktop, on demand on mobile.
- No post-processing. Physics only for the toys on the bed (the pillow and the UMich M / UW W / SJTU gear plushes: pick up, throw, knock into each other) and the hoops game: cannon-es in its own chunk (~24 KB gzip), loaded when the full-screen room first opens, bodies sleep when settled. The hoops game has its own world: static floor, walls, bed and a ring of 14 boxes for the bin, fixed 1/120 s steps, stepping only while a throw is awake. Real-time light (hemisphere + one shadowed sun/moon + night lamps); per-asset baked AO only. Render on demand; stop when off-screen or the tab is hidden.
- Light only comes in through the windows: walls (also the ones hidden in the dollhouse view), a permanent shadow-only ceiling seal and closed blinds cast shadows, so sun patches show only with a blind up. The floor lamp, bedside lamp and the two monitor light bars cast shadows too; the light bars follow the theme, no switch (2026-10-22). The shelf has a warm LED strip under every board: a line light written into the shelf's and walls' shaders (RectAreaLight would cost ~110 KB), so it lights each tier as an even band; on at night like the light bars.
- Wall cutaways apply to the page/outside overview. In a full-screen interior view (eye inside the L-shaped footprint and below the ceiling), keep every wall, corner post and wall-mounted object visible, plus a lit L-shaped ceiling underside at the physical 2.7 m height. Its permanent shadow seal is unchanged; the visible liner does not receive the seal’s self-shadow. The outside overview stays open-topped. Direction-based cutaways can otherwise expose the page background as black vertical bands beside the doors in wide/night views.
- Cache shadow depth maps while only the camera moves; keep all shadow-casting light counts stable across themes. Track old/new caster bounds and refresh only intersecting shadow lights after geometry changes; keep changes pending while a light is off. Plant/tapestry sway changes only visible-material shaders and redraws without refreshing depth maps. Hover uses at most one visible clickable-mesh query per animation frame, with opaque scene geometry retained as occluders.
- Full-room labels read all widths before updating positions to avoid repeated forced layouts. Performance stage 1 (2026-10-28) matched all 120 baseline image/crop comparisons and 39 cached-vs-forced shadow states; regressions verify sway does not regenerate shadows.
- Shadow maps keep only their depth textures: after preparation allocates every map, `dropShadowColor` (`room-shadows.ts`) detaches and deletes three's unused RGBA color texture (~33.5 MB at 1×), keeping the original attachment if the depth-only framebuffer were incomplete.
- `snack_stash` (behind the closed cabinet door) loads after the room is ready, placed hidden, prepared through the warmup queue for the canvas and mirror, then shown; it loads with the first screen when the room opens on Games or a `#room` link. First-screen GLBs are 6,186,504 bytes.
- Downloads start before synchronous environment preparation; meshopt uses up to two workers. `room-warmup.ts` queues texture uploads and submits material compilations before awaiting them. The default is a 4 ms budget and two jobs per turn; with input-pending detection and no recent input, use up to 12 ms and four jobs. Idle callbacks use a 34 ms timeout (timer fallback); a turn that fires on its timeout (idle callbacks starve while the page scrolls) still uses its full budget, and wheel scrolling does not count as input, since the compositor scrolls the page. Late assets (the snack stash) prepare their materials only: shadow maps already exist and must not be redrawn empty. At most four parallel compiles are in flight (a software GPU process queues them serially, blocking any synchronous GL call), and a timed-out turn's successor waits 34–120 ms according to the last turn's cost. Reflection variants are prepared before the canvas goes live (a failed preparation still reveals the mirror); until then the panel shows its still image with a "Building the room…" label, so nothing visible still needs compiling. Hoops props refresh shadows at most at 20 Hz while moving, always once more when they stop. The distance-map snapshot encodes with `toBlob` (object URLs, revoked on cache eviction), not a synchronous `toDataURL`. The hoops module prepares its held props, outline, guide dots and celebration materials through the same queue when it loads. Use `compileAsync` only with parallel shader compilation; actually draw each material group in a 1-pixel viewport. Allocate/clear each shadow depth texture without geometry before material preparation, including zero-brightness lights: an uninitialized comparison sampler can invalidate every lit draw. Canvas variants prepare behind the poster before going live; reflection-target variants prepare after 1.5 s before showing the mirror. A driver call cannot be preempted, so this bounds batches rather than guaranteeing an individual driver call fits the budget. Never compile/draw the whole scene synchronously at mount.
- Coffee steam and decorative light animation drive redraws only while visible; light visibility includes the illuminated region and mirror view. The clock still refreshes once per second. The distance map waits for room preparation when opened from the room, keeps full page/board snapshots and attribution, then removes its WebGL map; defer offscreen theme regeneration until idle or needed, and regenerate/cache snapshots on theme, size or pixel-ratio changes.
- Screen clips play in the page panel, redraw on decoded video frames (30 fps fallback), and pause in the full-screen room and every close-up. They resume on return to the page; hidden/off-screen panels also pause decoding. A paused clip alone must not keep the room rendering.
- All 3D assets ≤ ~3 MB (meshopt), room JS ≤ 200 KB gzip. Poster image fallback without WebGL or with reduced motion.
- Lighthouse home page: Performance ≥ 90 desktop / ≥ 80 mobile, Accessibility ≥ 95.

### Asset rules
- One collection per asset, `asset_<name>`; repeated items are one asset placed several times.
- Real scale (1 unit = 1 m), origin at the bottom center on the floor (wall-mounted: back-face bottom center), front faces −Y.
- Materials are roles: `frame`, `fabric`, `wood`, `metal`, `screen`, `glass`, `glow`, `accent`. Colors live in the `Color` vertex attribute; the config can recolor a role per placement.
- Surfaces that show page content get a flat plane with 0–1 UVs: `screen` (monitor, TV, 16:9), `cover` (game case), `board` (pinboard).
- Keep it light: no tiny details, bevel instead of subdividing.
- Bed: `bed_single` is placed; the queen `bed` is kept for later.
- `backpack` is the selected black commuter bag by MadeByYeshe (CC BY), resting beside the bed’s open long edge near its foot (+X). Keep this model and its 430 mm body/10° lean. `bin/room_backpack_asset.py` preserves the original UVs, 14,200 triangles and all seven source PBR images, rather than baking texture colours into vertices. Manifest `preserve_materials` bypasses role recolouring for this asset; `bin/room-pbr-textures.mjs` uses WebP quality 95 at the original resolution before meshopt (also called by `bin/room-assets`). Its GLB is about 1 MB; retaining the source appearance raises total GLBs to about 4.0 MB above the aspirational 3 MB budget. It stays clear of the bed/court and participates in hoops obstacles.

Preparation tests allow 120 s; first-visible-frame thresholds remain separately configurable.
